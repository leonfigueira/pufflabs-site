Copsemark privacy policy

Copsemark has no accounts, no servers of ours, and no analytics. Your surveys are files on your own device.

Last updated 6 September 2026.

The short version

We do not collect anything about you. There is nowhere for us to collect it to: Copsemark has no backend, no sign-in, no account, no advertising identifier, no crash reporter and no third-party analytics of any kind. Nothing you type, measure, photograph or export is sent to us, and we have no way to read any of it.

Where your surveys live

A survey is an ordinary file, the way a Pages document is. It holds the records you make, the base plan you imported and every photograph you took, all in one file. It goes wherever you save it: your device, iCloud Drive, or any other location the Files app offers. If you put it in iCloud Drive it is stored and synchronised by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, not by us. If you keep it on the device it stays on the device.

Delete the app and its surveys go with it, unless you saved them somewhere outside it. That is worth knowing before you need to know it.

What the app sends over the internet, and to whom

Copsemark is built to work with no signal, because a lot of surveying happens where there is none. Nothing you record needs the internet. Two things use it, both optional, and neither sends anything to us:

  • Planning constraint lookups. When you ask the app to check designations for a site, it queries public open-data services run by others: the government's own planning.data.gov.uk, NatureScot, and the equivalent services for the other UK nations. Those requests carry the area you asked about. They do not carry your name, your device identifier or anything about you. They go to those organisations, who have their own privacy policies, and never to us.
  • Purchases. Buying the app, or starting the free trial, goes through Apple's App Store. Apple tells the app one thing: whether this Apple Account has bought it. We never see your name, your address, your card, or how much you paid.

Permissions the app asks for, and why

  • Camera and photo library: to photograph a tree and attach it to its record. Photographs are stored inside the survey file and nowhere else.
  • Location: only to show where you are on the base plan while you work. Tree positions come from tapping the drawing, not from the device, because satellite positioning under a canopy is metres out. If you do record a position from the device, the app keeps the accuracy it reported and marks the record provisional until you check it. Your location is never sent anywhere.
  • Microphone and speech recognition: only if you use dictation for a note, so your hands can stay on the tape. Dictation is handled by iOS under Apple's terms.

Every one of these is optional, asked for at the moment it is needed, and the app works without it.

Children

Copsemark is a professional tool for arboricultural consultants. It is not directed at children and collects nothing from anybody.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have rights to see, correct and delete personal data an organisation holds about you. We hold none, so there is nothing to see, correct or delete. The exception is if you email us for support: we then have your email and whatever you wrote, we use it only to answer you, and you can ask us to delete it at any time.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and a material change will be described in the app's release notes rather than slipped in quietly.
